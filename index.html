Ride height

Target body height change at each corner, relative to design. The spring perches are set so the car settles there under its own weight. Four targets off one plane cannot all be met; the difference shows as cross weight.

Spring force at design length is the coilover force with the wheel at design height. A negative value means the spring is loose there and seats only in bump.

Model notes
  • Each corner is a rigid upright on two wishbones and a tie rod, solved in 3D. The wishbone pivot axes run parallel to the car's centreline, so camber, track, motion ratio and roll centre come from the front view. Caster, kingpin inclination, toe, bump steer and Ackermann come from the 3D solve.
  • Dynamics: body heave, pitch and roll (small angles) plus vertical motion of each wheel, and sideways slide and yaw while the car is driven. The body rolls about the roll axis, a tilted body carries its CG off-centre over the wheels, and the CG height follows the ride height.
  • Tires are thin discs with a linear vertical spring and light damping. They can leave the ground. Side force follows a Magic Formula curve from slip angle, load and camber: peak grip falls as load rises, cornering stiffness flattens with load, and camber leaning into the turn adds a little grip up to an optimum. The grip numbers are typical values, not test data, so trust the direction of a change more than the size.
  • Grip in use: the car is settled at each lateral g and each axle's side force is compared with the most its two tires can make at their loads, cambers and toes. The axle that reaches 100 % first sets the limit. The understeer gradient is the change in front minus rear slip angle between 0.2 and 0.4 g.
  • Assess turn-in (grip) (Steering tab): at the set steady speed the tires make the side force from their slip angles, the car slides sideways and yaws, and lateral g comes out of that. Speed is held constant, the road is flat, and there is no drive or brake force at the tires. Past the limit the car plows or spins; that part is the least accurate. Cornering loads do not jack the body.
  • No anti-dive or anti-squat: all longitudinal load transfer goes through the springs.
  • Lateral load transfer through the links uses each axle's roll centre height for equal travel on both sides and zero steer. The rear view shows the true roll centre for reference.
  • Steering lifts or lowers each front tire relative to its arm (caster and kingpin inclination), which shifts load diagonally. Springs, bump stops and anti-roll bars act on arm travel.
  • Coil springs cannot pull: they unseat at full droop. Anti-roll bar links are assumed adjusted for zero preload at the target heights.
  • Bump stops engage at the end of the published wheel stroke. The real car's stops touch earlier and build up progressively.
  • The Miata has no rear toe link. Its rear lower arm holds the upright at two outer pivots. The model draws that arm as two legs, the front leg to the ball joint and the rear leg to a second outer point, and the rear leg holds the toe. Both legs swing about the same axis, so the rear has no toe change by construction.
  • Left and right sides share one geometry, and the CG is on the centreline.
