Double Wishbone

Simulator / 9 DOF

Dimensions at design ride height. Lateral from the centerline, height from the ground, fore-aft from the axle line. The solver finds the lower-arm angle that puts the tire on the ground. Wheels and tires, coilover mounts and the tie rod are on their own tabs.

Solved points

At design height.

Values at the coilover. The motion ratio converts them to the wheel. Dampers are two-slope: the low-speed slope up to the knee, the high-speed slope beyond it.

Ride height

Target body height change at each corner, relative to design. The spring perches are set so the car settles there under its own weight. Four targets off one plane cannot all be met; the difference shows as cross weight.

Spring force at design length is the coilover force with the wheel at design height. A negative value means the spring is loose there and seats only in bump.

Steering wheel

Cut knuckles is off in both cars; the 20 mm is a placeholder, so enter what was cut from yours. Caster and static toe are on the Geometry tab. The rack follows the steering wheel at up to 900°/s.

Model notes
  • Each corner is a rigid upright on two wishbones and a tie rod, solved in 3D. The wishbone pivot axes run parallel to the car's centerline, so camber, track, motion ratio and roll center come from the front view. Caster, kingpin inclination, toe, bump steer and Ackermann come from the 3D solve. The wheel is fixed on the knuckle: camber is the knuckle's own angle minus the kingpin inclination the arms give, so arm lengths and pivots set it, and a camber adjuster adds at most +1° or −3° on top. Wheel offset and spacers slide the wheel along the spindle. A change of tire radius raises or lowers the car and leaves the suspension where it was.
  • Dynamics: body heave, pitch and roll (small angles) plus vertical motion of each wheel, and sideways slide and yaw while the car is driven. The body rolls about the roll axis, a tilted body carries its CG off-center over the wheels, and the CG height follows the ride height.
  • Tires are thin discs with a linear vertical spring and light damping. They can leave the ground. Side force follows a Magic Formula curve from slip angle, load and camber: peak grip falls as load rises, cornering stiffness flattens with load, and camber leaning into the turn adds a little grip up to an optimum. The grip numbers are typical values, not test data, so trust the direction of a change more than the size.
  • Grip in use: the car is settled at each lateral g and each axle's side force is compared with the most its two tires can make at their loads, cambers and toes. The axle that reaches 100 % first sets the limit. The understeer gradient is the change in front minus rear slip angle between 0.2 and 0.4 g.
  • Drive (the button at the top; its speed and steering controls are on the Forces tab): at the set steady speed the tires make the side force from their slip angles, the car slides sideways and yaws, and lateral g comes out of that. Speed is held constant, the road is flat, and there is no drive or brake force at the tires. Past the limit the car plows or spins; that part is the least accurate. Cornering loads do not jack the body. While it is on, the floor grid in the 3D view moves under the car: it streams back at the car's speed, turns as the car yaws, and drifts sideways when the car slides. Finer grid lines fade out at speed so the motion stays readable.
  • No anti-dive or anti-squat: all longitudinal load transfer goes through the springs.
  • Lateral load transfer through the links uses each axle's roll center height for equal travel on both sides and zero steer. The rear view shows the true roll center for reference.
  • Steering lifts or lowers each front tire relative to its arm (caster and kingpin inclination), which shifts load diagonally. Springs, bump stops and anti-roll bars act on arm travel.
  • Coil springs cannot pull: they unseat at full droop. Anti-roll bar links are assumed adjusted for zero preload at the target heights.
  • Bump stops engage at the end of the published wheel stroke. The real car's stops touch earlier and build up progressively.
  • The Miata has no rear toe link. Its rear lower arm holds the upright at two outer pivots. The model draws that arm as two legs, the front leg to the ball joint and the rear leg to a second outer point, and the rear leg holds the toe. Both legs swing about the same axis, so the rear has no toe change by construction.
  • Left and right sides share one geometry, and the CG is on the centerline.
  • The car bodies and rims in the 3D view are a drawing only. They are reduced from Assetto Corsa car models: the Mazda Roadster 1.8 RS (NB) and the Mazda Roadster (NB8C) Garage Vary by Sam S., and a rim from the ADC Mazda MX-5 NB Stock by Aussie Drift Co. Each wheel is drawn to its axle's rim diameter, rim width, offset, tire width and loaded radius.
