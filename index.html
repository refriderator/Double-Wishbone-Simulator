Model notes
  • Each corner is a rigid knuckle on two wishbones and a tie rod, solved in 3D. Each wishbone swings about its own pivot axis, which can be angled in side view and in top view. Camber, caster, kingpin inclination, toe, bump steer, Ackermann, track, motion ratio and roll center all come from that one solve.
  • The wheel is fixed on the knuckle, so the arms set the camber. The hub angle adds +1° to −3°: it stands in for the car's eccentric bolts. Caster follows from where the two ball joints sit along their arms. The tie rod's length sets static toe. Offset and spacers slide the wheel along the spindle. A tire radius change moves the car, not the suspension.
  • The solver finds the smallest change to the ticked fields that puts the targets on the setup sheet at ride height. It gives no answer when there are more targets than ticked fields, when those fields cannot move the targets independently, when nothing within the fields' limits gets there, or when the car could not be built with the answer.
  • Dynamics: body heave, pitch and roll (small angles), each wheel's vertical motion, and sideways slide and yaw while driven. The body rolls about the roll axis, and the CG height follows ride height.
  • In a corner the links carry part of the load transfer straight to the tires: the body's share at the roll center height, the wheels' own share at a height the same geometry sets, below the wheel center. The rest goes through springs and anti-roll bars. Braking and accelerating load transfer all goes through the springs. Cornering forces do not lift or lower the body (no jacking).
  • Roll center: from the arms alone, wheel held straight, equal travel on both sides. The rear view shows the roll center of the car as it sits.
  • Anti-dive is on the setup sheet only. It needs an angled pivot axis, and the dynamics do not use it. No anti-squat.
  • Tires are thin discs with a linear vertical spring and light damping. They can leave the ground. Side force follows a Magic Formula curve of slip angle, load and camber. The grip numbers are typical values, not test data: trust the direction of a change more than its size.
  • Grip in use: the car is settled at each lateral g and each axle's side force is compared with the most its tires can make. The axle that reaches 100 % first sets the limit. The understeer gradient is the change in front minus rear slip angle from 0.2 to 0.4 g.
  • Drive: constant speed, flat road, no drive or brake force at the tires. The tires make the side force from their slip angles, and the car slides and yaws. Past the limit it plows or spins; that part is the least accurate.
  • Steering lifts or lowers each front tire against its arm (caster, kingpin inclination), which shifts load diagonally. The rack follows the steering wheel at up to 900°/s.
  • Springs cannot pull: they unseat at full droop. Bump stops start at the bump travel limit. Anti-roll bars carry no preload at the target heights.
  • The Miata has no rear toe link: its rear lower arm holds the knuckle at two outer pivots. The model treats that arm as two legs on one axis, so the rear has no toe change. The rear's pivot axes stay parallel to the centerline.
  • Left and right share one geometry, and the CG is on the centerline.
