Target body height change at each corner, relative to design. The spring perches are set so the car settles there under its own weight. The body is rigid: four targets off one plane cannot all be met. The car settles between them and the difference shows as cross weight.

Spring force at design length is the coilover force with the wheel at design height. A negative value means the spring is loose there and seats only in bump.

Sprung mass is everything the springs carry. Unsprung mass is wheel, tire, upright, brake and about half the arms and coilover. Roll inertia is about the CG. The model adds the offset to the roll axis. CG height follows the ride height.

Model notes
  • Each corner is a rigid upright on two wishbones and a tie rod, solved in 3D. The wishbone pivot axes run parallel to the car's centreline, so camber, track, motion ratio and roll centre come from the front view. Caster, kingpin inclination, toe, bump steer and Ackermann come from the 3D solve.
  • Dynamics: body heave, pitch and roll (small angles) plus vertical motion of each wheel. The body rolls about the roll axis, a tilted body carries its CG off-centre over the wheels, and the CG height follows the ride height.
  • Tires are thin discs with a linear vertical spring and light damping. They can leave the ground. There are no lateral or longitudinal tire forces, so camber and toe do not change grip and cornering loads do not jack the body.
  • Lateral g from steering is optional. It uses the low-speed turn radius (tires roll where they point, no slip angle) and is capped at the grip limit.
  • No anti-dive or anti-squat: all longitudinal load transfer goes through the springs.
  • Lateral load transfer through the links uses each axle's roll centre height for equal travel on both sides and zero steer. The rear view shows the true roll centre for reference.
  • Steering lifts or lowers each front tire relative to its arm (caster and kingpin inclination), which shifts load diagonally. Springs, bump stops and anti-roll bars act on arm travel.
  • Coil springs cannot pull: they unseat at full droop. Anti-roll bar links are assumed adjusted for zero preload at the target heights.
  • Bump stops engage at the end of the published wheel stroke. The real car's stops touch earlier and build up progressively.
  • The Miata has no rear toe link. Its rear lower arm holds the upright at two outer pivots. The model draws that arm as two legs, the front leg to the ball joint and the rear leg to a second outer point, and the rear leg holds the toe. Both legs swing about the same axis, so the rear has no toe change by construction.
  • Left and right sides share one geometry, and the CG is on the centreline.
